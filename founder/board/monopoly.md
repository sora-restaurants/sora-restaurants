# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- A clear small-market wedge exists on paper: independent sit-down restaurants in South Africa that want table ordering without a POS migration, new hardware, or a per-transaction commission. "No POS replacement" is a real positioning choice, not a feature.
- The team is the right shape for the stage: two builders and one person who sells and trains, so distribution is staffed from day one.
- Founder-led training on site is the seed of know-how and a local brand that a foreign or self-serve competitor would not replicate cheaply.
- The engineering basics (server-side pricing, tenant data separation, walk-out policies) are done before any customer, which lowers pilot risk.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage.** QR menus and ordering are an incremental improvement, not a tenfold one. There is no proprietary technology, no network effect, no scale economy yet. A POS vendor or payment provider can bundle the same thing for free, and a copycat can match the demo by month six.
2. **Distribution does not scale.** Direct sales plus in-person training by one founder sets a hard ceiling on restaurants per month. Cost to acquire a restaurant, churn and support cost are all unknown, so nobody can yet say whether one restaurant at R299-R1,299 pays back its acquisition.
3. **Unknown competition in a likely crowded market.** No competitor map exists. If several SA and global QR-ordering products already sell to the same owners, this is a fight on price with thin margins, the exact situation the lens warns against.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who already sells QR ordering or table service to SA restaurants, and at what price?
- What is the one small market you could own most of in 12 months: a city, a segment (wine-farm restaurants, one franchise group), or a single POS's users?
- What is your secret: what do you know about these owners that competitors do not?
- Which advantage can this build over ten years: POS integration depth, a brand, data across restaurants, or something else?
- How do the first 100 restaurants arrive, and what does each one cost in founder hours and money?
- What stops a POS vendor or payment gateway from giving this away in month six?
- Why now: what has changed in SA restaurants that makes this the right moment?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A competitor map of SA table-ordering options with prices, before more building.
2. One named beachhead segment or area, with an estimate of how many restaurants are in it.
3. At least one real pilot restaurant live, with measured onboarding time and support load.
4. A first estimate of cost to acquire a restaurant and the payback period.
5. A written answer to "what stops a copycat", tied to one advantage the plan will deliberately build (for example, deep integration with the POS most of the beachhead uses).
