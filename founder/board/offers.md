# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Low effort for the buyer: no new hardware, no POS migration, no app download, runs on phones and tablets the restaurant already owns. That covers the effort side of the value equation well.
- Fast time to a result, in principle: a QR menu and kitchen screen could be live in days, and staff training comes with every plan.
- No per-transaction commission is an honest point of difference against anyone who takes a cut. The value of that is untested.
- Pay-now or pay-after policies and the unpaid-orders list speak to a real fear, walk-outs. That is the kind of problem that belongs in the stack.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Zero likelihood proof.** No restaurant has used it, there are no interviews and no pilot. Perceived likelihood is where most offers fail, and today it sits near zero. An owner is being asked to trust table ordering on a Friday night to software that has only run on made-up data.
2. **Unproven market.** Nobody knows how big the pain is, whether owners will spend R699 a month, how hard they are to reach outside founder-led sales, or whether the segment is growing. A weak market makes this lens pass whatever the offer is.
3. **A commodity offer.** As written, it is a feature list at a monthly price. The dream outcome (more covers, bigger orders, fewer walk-outs, fewer staff hours) is never stated or measured. Competitors and their prices are unknown, so the risk is a price fight nobody has mapped.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which owner, exactly, loses the most money from slow ordering, and how much does it cost them a month?
- What result will you promise, in rands or minutes, and how will you prove it after 30 days?
- What guarantee can you afford? For example: no result in 60 days, money back.
- Who already sells QR ordering in South Africa, and at what price?
- Could you charge R1,400 for Pro and still be the obvious choice? If not, why not?
- What do payment processing, SMS and support cost per restaurant? Unknown.
- What real scarcity can you offer, such as a fixed number of founding pilot slots because three founders can only train so many?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Run 3 to 5 pilot restaurants on real services and measure one outcome before and after, such as order time, average bill or walk-outs.
2. Rebuild the offer around that measured outcome: a named result, onboarding done for them, a guarantee, and capped founding slots.
3. Map the South African competitors and their prices before a price is fixed.
4. Choose a payment gateway and a real POS integration for the pilot's own POS, so the offer is honest about what works today.
