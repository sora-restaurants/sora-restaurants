# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The core moment is clear and good: a diner scans, orders and tracks without downloading an app, and the kitchen sees it straight away. The product starts from what the customer experiences, not from the technology.
- It sits alongside the restaurant's POS, needs no new hardware and runs on phones and tablets the restaurant already has. That makes switching easy for the owner.
- The parts nobody sees have had care: prices are set on the server, duplicate orders are blocked, each restaurant's data is kept separate and tested on a real database, and owners choose a pay-now or pay-after policy to limit walk-outs.
- No commission per order keeps the promise to owners simple.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Gaps where the order passes between systems.** The order goes from diner to kitchen screen to POS to payment to receipt, and today the POS link, the payments and the printing are all simulated or missing. In a busy service the order is most likely to go wrong in one of these gaps: it never reaches the till, the bill does not match, or nothing prints. That failure lands on the owner's own staff.
2. **No focus.** There are three plans, a branded web page, delivery links, ordering, waiter calls, analytics and POS integrations. The one thing Sora must do brilliantly has not been named, and the R299 Starter plan (a web page) does not use the ordering product at all.
3. **No real restaurant has used it.** Nobody has seen the first five minutes for a diner or the first busy Friday night for a kitchen. The design rests on guesses about how staff, waiters and diners actually behave.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one thing, in one sentence? If you could keep only one screen, which would it be?
- Walk me through the first five minutes for a diner at a braai spot with weak signal. What happens when the phone drops the connection in the middle of an order?
- When a QR order is accepted, how does it get into the POS and onto the bill today, and who fixes it when it does not?
- What would you cut? Why does the Starter plan exist?
- Who owns setup, menu entry and support after training, and how long does it take for one restaurant? (Unknown.)
- Which payment gateway will you use, and what does a diner see when a payment fails?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name the one thing in a single sentence, and cut or park everything that does not serve it (starting with Starter and the Premium analytics).
2. Run one pilot restaurant through real busy services, with a real payment gateway and a working path for each order to reach the POS or the bill. Report the orders that went wrong and what caused each one.
3. Write down the full experience a diner and a waiter have from end to end, including when payment fails, the signal drops or a dish has sold out, and decide who owns each step.
