# Board verdict: Sora

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass. Average score 4.7 / 10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 5).
Memos: `founder/board/offers.md`, `monopoly.md`, `product.md`. Brief: `founder/board/brief.md`.

The board is not saying no. It is saying the software is ahead of the business: the build is careful, but nothing
about the market, the customer or the money has been tested yet.

## Risks more than one member raised (most serious first)

1. **No real restaurant has used it.** (All three.) Every judgement about owners, staff and diners is a guess until a
   restaurant runs real services on it. Offers calls this "zero likelihood proof"; Product says nobody has seen the
   first busy Friday night.
2. **Unknown, probably crowded competition.** (Offers, Monopoly.) There is no map of who already sells QR ordering to
   South African restaurants or at what price. Without it, the R299 / R699 / R1,299 prices are guesses and the risk
   is a price fight.
3. **Nothing stops a copy or a free bundle.** (Monopoly, Offers.) QR ordering is an incremental improvement; a POS
   vendor or payment provider could include it for free. The plan names no advantage that grows over time.
4. **The links that make it real are simulated.** (Product, Offers.) Payments, the POS connection and printing are
   stand-ins. In a busy service, orders go wrong exactly where they pass between systems.

## Where the members disagree

- **What to build next.** Product wants focus and cuts (drop Starter, park Premium analytics). Monopoly wants an
  advantage deliberately built, for example deep integration with one POS. Offers wants the offer rebuilt around a
  measured result. They agree on the order: prove it in a pilot first.
- **The Starter plan.** Product says cut it (a web page, not the ordering product). Offers would rather sharpen the
  offer than remove tiers. Unresolved; `/founder-pricing` and `/founder-offer` should settle it.
- **No-commission pricing.** Offers counts it as a real difference worth testing; Monopoly notes it is easy for a
  competitor to match.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] Map South African table-ordering competitors and their prices. → `/founder-competitors`
- [ ] Name one beachhead (a city, a segment such as wine-farm restaurants, or the users of one POS) and estimate how
      many restaurants are in it. → `/founder-competitors`, `/founder-marketing`
- [ ] Name the one thing Sora must do brilliantly, in one sentence; park what does not serve it. → `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Decide Starter's fate and set prices against competitors and buyer answers. → `/founder-consumer`, `/founder-pricing`
- [ ] Choose a payment gateway, and give each order a working path to the pilot's POS or bill. → `/founder-ops`
- [ ] Write the full diner and waiter journey, including payment failing, the signal dropping and sold-out dishes,
      with an owner for each step. → `/founder-ops`
- [ ] Run 3 to 5 pilot restaurants on real services; measure one outcome before and after (order time, average bill,
      walk-outs), plus onboarding time and support load; log every order that went wrong and why. → `/founder-launch`
- [ ] Rebuild the offer around the measured result: a named outcome, done-for-you setup, an affordable guarantee,
      capped founding slots. → `/founder-offer`
- [ ] First estimate of the cost to win a restaurant, support cost per restaurant and payback period. → `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] A written answer to "what stops a copycat", tied to one advantage the plan will deliberately build. → `/founder-plan`

## The strongest version the board can see

Not "a restaurant platform with three plans", but one thing done better than anyone in one place: **table
ordering that works alongside the POS a specific group of South African restaurants already uses, set up and
trained in person, with no commission**. Pick the beachhead by the POS it runs (so the integration becomes the
advantage a copycat cannot cheaply match), prove one measurable result in a handful of pilot restaurants, and sell
that result with founding slots and a guarantee. The board would fund that business. This differs from the pitch:
it narrows the customer, demotes the Starter web page and the analytics tier, and moves the POS integration from
"Premium, later" to the centre of the plan.
