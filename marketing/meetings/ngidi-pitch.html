SORA · Meeting pitch · Private, for the SORA team only

Pitching Sora to Andile and Ayanda Ngidi

La Vie Hospitality Group: Donna Modern Italian, Butcher & Wine, Saint Yves and Kung Thai, at the New Salt Rock Centre, Salt Rock.

The one goal

Leave with a date. Agree to set Sora up at one venue (Saint Yves or Donna) for two to four weeks, as a Founding Restaurant, then roll it out across the group if it works.

Don't try to sell all four venues in one meeting. Sell the first one.

Who you're talking to

  • Twin brothers from Durban. They started as waiters and bartenders, ran an events company, then co-founded The Danté Experience in Umhlanga (a restaurant, five bars and a rooftop) and sold it in 2022.
  • Now they run four venues side by side in Salt Rock as one "something for everyone" hub. Kung Thai is a franchise they run as part of La Vie.
  • Ayanda handles numbers and admin: show him the dashboard and the cost. Andile is the people person: show him what changes for guests and staff.
  • They're operators and dealmakers. Talk table turns, labour, walk-outs and Friday nights, not technology.

Before you go

  • Visit Saint Yves and Donna on a Friday night. Time how long it takes to order, get a drink and get the bill. Mention what you saw.
  • Know how you price. Each venue is priced by its size: more tables means more QR codes to set up and more to maintain. Have a rough range ready (for a smaller and a bigger venue) so you can answer "roughly how much?", and offer an exact quote per venue once you've counted their tables. Don't make up an exact number on the spot.
  • Demo ready: laptop or tablet as the kitchen screen, a printed QR on the table, your phone's hotspot as a backup. Run it once the night before.
  • Bring this sheet and a one-page summary to leave with them.

The meeting, step by step (about 15 minutes)

1. Open with their world (1 minute)

"You two started out on the floor, so you know this better than I do. On a busy Friday at Saint Yves, what goes wrong first: getting orders in, getting drinks out, or getting people to pay?"

Then stop and listen. Write down their exact words; you'll use them later.

2. Ask questions (3 minutes)

  • How do orders get from the table to the bar and kitchen today?
  • What happens on your busiest nights? Where do things slow down?
  • Do you lose money to walk-outs or open tabs at Saint Yves?
  • How many staff on a Friday shift? Is it hard to find good waiters?
  • Which POS do you use, and is it the same in all four venues?

3. The demo (5 minutes)

Put the QR on the table and hand them the phone. Let them do it.

  1. They scan the QR. The menu opens in the browser, with no app to download.
  2. They order a cocktail and a starter, and tap Send.
  3. The order appears on the kitchen/bar screen straight away. You tap it to Ready.
  4. Their phone shows the order is ready.
  5. They tap Call waiter, and it appears on the waiter screen with the table number.
  6. Show the owner dashboard: orders and value for the night.
"That's it. The guest orders when they're ready, not when a waiter is free. Your staff spend their time serving and selling, not running orders back and forth."

4. Tie it to what they told you (3 minutes)

Use their own words from step 1. For example:

"You said Friday nights at the bar get backed up. With Sora, every table can order at the same time, and the bartender sees it all in one list."

5. Be honest about where Sora is (1 minute)

"We're new. You'd be one of our first founding restaurants. That's why the price is discounted, and why you'd have a direct line to us: what you ask for, we build."

6. The offer (1 minute)

  • A Founding Restaurant place: discounted introductory pricing, paid, quoted per venue by its size (tables and QR codes).
  • We set it up on site and train your staff ourselves.
  • No commission on orders.
  • Your feedback shapes the product.

7. The close (1 minute)

"Let's prove it at one venue first. Can we set up at Saint Yves on [date] and run it through two Friday nights? If it doesn't help, you've lost nothing but two weeks."

If they hesitate, ask: "What would you need to see to say yes?" Then stay quiet.

How to explain paying (keep it simple)

Each venue picks its own rule:

  • Pay after the meal: works today. Guests pay the normal way, with your card machine, at the till or in cash. Sora shows the waiter which tables haven't paid yet, and the waiter taps "Mark paid".
  • Pay when ordering: the order only reaches the kitchen once it's paid, which stops walk-outs. This is not live yet. It needs a payment provider connected.
"Today your staff keep using your own card machines. Sora never touches your money. Pay-before-you-order is our next step, once a payment provider is connected."

If they want a group deal or a stake

They've bought and sold venues, so they might ask. Decide in advance what you'd accept for all four venues and whether you're open to talking about a stake. If you're not ready, say:

"Let's prove it at one venue first. If it works, we'll put a group offer in front of you."
